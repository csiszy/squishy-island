Accounts & saving

Play now, save later

Nobody has to sign up to play. The moment the game is first opened it quietly creates a guest account and saves the town to the cloud. Later, at moments when the town clearly matters to the child, Bao asks them to get a grown-up to save the town with an email address (or Apple / Google sign-in). That is the Hay Day / Supercell ID pattern, adjusted for a kids’ game: the grown-up does the linking, never the child.

Recommendation

How the account works

1 · Guest account from the first second

  • On first launch the game creates an anonymous account: a random player ID plus a secret key kept in the phone’s secure storage (iOS Keychain, Android Keystore with Block Store backup). No screens, no questions.
  • Progress saves to the server a few seconds after every change and when the app goes to the background, with a copy on the phone for offline play.
  • On iPhone the Keychain usually survives deleting and reinstalling the app, so even a guest often gets their town back on the same phone.
  • What a guest can lose: the town, if the phone is lost, broken or reset. That is exactly the story the save prompts tell.

2 · Linking (“Save my town”)

  • Always starts with the grown-up gate.
  • Email with a 6-digit code (no password to forget): works on every phone, and is the main option.
  • Sign in with Apple on iPhone/iPad and Google on Android as quick extras. If Google sign-in is offered on iPhone too, Apple also requires Sign in with Apple (or a similar privacy-friendly option).
  • The email belongs to the parent, is used only to send codes and bring the town back, and never for marketing. A confirmation email tells the parent what is stored and how to delete it.
  • Linked accounts show “Saved ✓” in Settings and the cloud icon on the map turns green.

Try it

The save flow

When Bao asks

A small orange dot sits on the cloud icon (top bar of the map) until the town is saved. The full “Keep your town safe!” card appears only at moments that matter:

  • the first house reaches level 3 (usually day 1–2)
  • the first Epic or Legendary squishy
  • the 3rd day the game is opened
  • right after any purchase (always: bought things must never be lost)
  • a player with 20+ squishies who still isn’t saved: once a week

At most one card a day. After the child taps “Later” five times, only the dot stays. Never during a minigame, never in the first 10 minutes of the first day.

Thank-you gift

Saving gives the town a Safety Cloud weathervane for the roof of the Steamhouse. Decoration only: no Gems or coins, so nobody feels pushed to hand over an email for currency.

Delete account

Required by both Apple and Google: in the app (behind the gate) and on a web page. Shows what will be lost, asks once more, then the account is deleted after a 30-day grace period (signing in during that time cancels it). Purchases are lost too, and the screen says so.

Kids, privacy and the law

Keep the data tiny

What the game stores

  • A random player ID, the town (squishies, levels, coins, Gems), settings, purchase receipts.
  • Player names come from word lists (“Bouncy Bao 482”): no free typing anywhere, so no real names and nothing to moderate.
  • No chat, no photos, no location, no contacts, no advertising ID.
  • The parent’s email only if they link it.
  • Play statistics only first-party and grouped (how many players finish level 10), never sold or shared.

Rules to check with a lawyer

  • US (COPPA): collecting personal information (an email counts) from children under 13 needs verifiable parental consent, with a few narrow exceptions. Treat every player as a child.
  • EU/UK (GDPR): parental consent below the national age of digital consent (16 in Hungary, 13–16 elsewhere). A plain-language privacy policy written for parents and a kid-friendly version.
  • Apple and Google require in-app account deletion, and Google also a deletion web page.
  • Store programmes: Apple’s Kids Category and Google’s Designed for Families limit third-party SDKs (analytics, ads, sign-in). Pick every SDK from their approved lists.

This is a design recommendation, not legal advice.

For the builder

  • The chosen backend is Nakama on an EU server (see the build plan): it has guest accounts, account linking, cloud saves, wallets and Google purchase checking built in.
  • Keep coins, Gems and purchases on the server. The server checks that minigame earnings are believable (no more than about 60 coins per minute of reported play), so a hacked phone can’t mint Gems.
  • Offline play is fine: earnings sync when the phone is back online, within the same limits.
  • Built (Step 4b): cloud save, email codes, transfer code + PIN, “Which town?”, delete with 30 days’ grace, the grown-up gate. Until an email service is chosen at launch, codes go to the server’s log (a test server also shows them on the phone). Google/Apple sign-in, Bao’s save cards and the Safety Cloud gift come later.
  • Purchases are also restorable from the App Store / Play account (“Restore purchases” in Settings).
