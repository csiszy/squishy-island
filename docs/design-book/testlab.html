Squishy Testing Facility

A shiny white lab where scientists test how squishy every squishy is. There is no score and no coins here: it is a place to squish, stretch, drop and press your squishies just because it feels good. It grows from a single test bench into a lab with a giant press on the roof.

  • Address: 1 Mochi Lane

  • Toy: Squish Lab
  • Coins: none, it's just for fun

The toy

Squish Lab

▶ Try the prototypeWorks on phone and computer: play/squish-lab.html

Pick any squishy you own and put it on the test table in a little 3D lab. Every squishy is a real soft body: its outline is traced from its own picture, it keeps its volume, remembers its shape and springs back, and its picture stretches and bulges with it. Nothing ever breaks: squishies always recover.

Three kinds of squish

  • Slow-rise foam (bao, toast, peach, marshmallow…): sinks in when you press and slowly rises back over about two seconds, like real slow-rise squishies.
  • Wobbly jelly (jelly, axolotl, whale, gummy…): keeps its volume, so it bulges out at the sides and wobbles.
  • Bouncy rubber (kitty, puppy, bunny, egg…): springy and bounces when dropped.

Testing tools

  1. Hand: pull an edge to stretch it (two fingers stretch both ways; pull far enough and it pops free), grab the middle to pick it up and throw it, tap to poke a dent.
  2. Press: a hydraulic press comes down while you hold and lifts when you let go. It always stops before the squishy gets too thin.
  3. Roller: drag a rolling pin over it to flatten it.
  4. Squeeze: two paddles squeeze it from both sides, so it grows tall.
  5. Ball: tap to drop a heavy rubber ball on it.
  6. Drop: lifts it high and lets go. SPLAT!

The Squish Run (in the real game)

Throw the squishy up out of the picture and the camera follows it up a wind tunnel to a marble run above the lab. On the way down it squeezes through a narrow chute, gets pulled between two turning gears whose teeth mesh close together, lands on a trapdoor where two automatic paddles squeeze it twice, bounces off pinball bumpers and drops back onto the table (about 6 to 10 seconds). Nobody can touch it in there, so nothing in the run can hold it: the run’s walls have no grip, the gears always turn it downwards, the trapdoor always opens, and a squishy that stops making its way down is let through. Every squish on the way counts on the squish-o-meter.

Squish-o-meter

A meter at the top shows how squished the squishy is right now, and the best squish so far. It is just for fun.

Physics, briefly

Each squishy is a ring of 40 points with stretchy skin, a volume it keeps, and a shape it remembers. Foam's remembered shape gives in while it is squished and only creeps back slowly (that is the slow rise); jelly and rubber spring straight back. The table and the press have grip, so squishies don't slide away while you press.

3D look, 2D physics

Everything you see is 3D: a lab room with a tiled wall, a test table, a chrome hydraulic press, a wooden rolling pin, yellow squeeze paddles, rubber balls, lights and soft shadows. All the squishing still happens in a flat 2D plane (left-right and up-down), which keeps it fast and rock-steady. The squishy itself is a puffy 3D pillow built around its 2D outline, with its picture stretched over the front; when it is squished flatter it puffs out thicker toward you.

Rewards

None on purpose: the lab never pays coins. Building levels unlock more tools (see below), so the lab grows as you play with it.

For the real app

Which engine, and how to keep it fast

The game is built in Godot 4 (see the build plan), and the Lab is one of its sealed minigames. The physics advice below holds whatever the engine.

  • Keep this exact physics model, don't swap in a big 3D soft-body engine. Port the prototype's solver (a ring of 40–60 points with skin springs, volume, shape memory and memory-foam creep) to GDScript. General 3D soft-body tools (for example Obi Softbody or finite-element plugins) are far heavier, harder to keep stable and not needed here.
  • It's cheap. One squishy is about 40 points × 600 physics steps a second: well under 1 millisecond per frame on a phone. Run it on a fixed timestep only while the Lab is open, so it never slows the rest of the app; if a cheap phone ever struggles, that one loop can move to C++ (GDExtension).
  • Use the real 3D squishy. Instead of a picture on a pillow, put the same 3D squishy model from the city in a simple deformation cage driven by the 2D outline: every vertex follows the soft body left-right and up-down, and puffs out front-to-back when squished. It looks fully 3D while the physics stays 2D.
  • Feel. 60 frames a second, a short phone buzz on impacts, and a few recorded squelch and squeak sounds whose pitch and volume follow how fast the squishy is being squished.

Building levels

From empty plot to level 10

The plot is fixed on the map and shows a “Build me!” sign until you pay to build level 1. After that, each upgrade unlocks after a number of squishes in the Lab (a squish counts when the squish-o-meter goes past 50%), and then costs coins. Each level also unlocks a new tool, so the lab is a little coin sink, never a coin source. Tap any level to see it bigger.

  1. Test bench. A little bench with a measuring board. Tools: Hand and Drop.
  2. Bench + chart. A wall chart of squish scores. Tool: Ball.
  3. Little lab. A small white lab with a blue roof and a mini press out front. Tool: Press.
  4. Awnings. Blue awnings over the windows. Tool: Roller.
  5. Wider lab. Big windows and a satellite dish on the roof. Tool: Squeeze.
  6. Second floor. An upstairs test room. Lets you test two squishies at once.
  7. Bubble tank. A bubbling glass tank with a jelly floating inside.
  8. Lit sign & garden. Bulbs, flowers and a fence. New table: trampoline.
  9. Giant press. A giant press squishes a giant marshmallow on the roof.
  10. Golden Lab. Flags, star medal and sparkles.
