Sound & music

Everything squeaks

Every tap gets a soft answer, every reward has its own little tune, and each place in town has its own background song. The sound should feel like a toy box: round, bouncy and friendly. Nothing is harsh, nothing is scary, and nothing is loud enough to startle a child or a parent sitting next to them.

  • Sound effects: 38 (all playable below)
  • Music: 14 looping tracks
  • Settings: effects, music and vibration each switch off separately (Settings)

In this design bookEvery sound here is made live in the browser, so you can hear it right now and the prototypes stay single files. The real game should use produced versions of the same ideas: real squishy-toy recordings for the squelches, and a composer’s versions of the tunes.

Results screens

Counters that count

Numbers on a results screen never just appear. Each one runs up from 0 to its value in half a second (fast at first, slowing at the end), with a tick for every step whose pitch rises as it climbs, and a bright ding when it lands. Coins clink instead of tick. Tapping the screen while it counts jumps straight to the end, so impatient players never wait. With Reduced animations on, numbers appear at once with a single ding.

Background music

A song for every place

Each place has its own looping song. They all share the same cheerful sound (plucks, bells, soft drums) so the town feels like one world, but each has its own tempo and mood. Tap one to listen; tap again to stop. Songs fade in and out over about a second when you move between places.

Music rules

  • Music starts at 50% volume, effects at 80%. Music always sits under the effects.
  • Music ducks (gets quieter) during results screens, ads, the story narration and Bao’s voice lines, then comes back.
  • If the player is already listening to their own music or a podcast, the game music stays off and only effects play (iOS “ambient” audio, Android audio focus).
  • The iPhone silent switch mutes the game.
  • Squeak Beat is the exception: its music is the game, so it follows the sound effects switch and plays no background track.
  • Real game: each song is a 1–2 minute seamless loop, and the city theme’s melody reappears in other tracks so the music feels like one family.

Effect rules

  • Every tap on anything tappable makes a sound. A tap that can’t do anything (locked, not enough coins) gets the soft “uh-uh”, never silence.
  • The same sound never plays twice within 40 ms, and frequent sounds (taps, coins) shift pitch a little each time so they never get tiring.
  • At most 8 sounds at once; the quietest is dropped first.
  • Nothing peaks louder than the Legendary fanfare. Thunder in the story is a far-away rumble, never a crack.
  • The rarer the box reveal, the longer and fancier its tune, so kids can hear what they got before they see it.

Free to use

Who owns these sounds

These are yours

Every effect and song on this page is made by code written for this game (art/sound.js): no samples, recordings or someone else’s music. So there is nothing to license and nothing to pay. They can be used, changed and sold in the game freely, with no credit needed.

They also exist as ready audio files for the real game (WAV effects and seamless Ogg music loops, rendered straight from this code), so the real game just plays the files.

If you add free sounds from elsewhere

  • OK for a game with ads and purchases: CC0 / public domain (for example Kenney’s game audio packs, CC0 sounds on Freesound or OpenGameArt), the Pixabay Content Licence, and CC BY (only with a credit in Settings → Credits, e.g. Kevin MacLeod’s music).
  • Not OK: anything marked NC (“non-commercial”): the game earns money. Also not the YouTube Audio Library (licensed for YouTube videos only) or music ripped from videos or games.
  • Keep a small list of where every outside sound came from and its licence, in case a store or a lawyer asks.
