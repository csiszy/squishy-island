Settings

The gear button in the top-right corner of the city map opens Settings. Kids can change the everyday things (sound, music, vibration, graphics). Anything to do with money, ads, trading, notifications, play time or deleting the account sits behind the grown-up gate.

These switches really workThe phone below saves to this browser, and every prototype reads it: switch off music and the minigames go quiet, pick Low graphics and they draw at a lower resolution, turn off Gem pop-ups (grown-ups only) and the Factory’s Gem Shop stops asking.

Settings×

How the screen behaves

  • One scrolling list with big rows, an icon on every row (for kids who can’t read yet) and a short line under each name.
  • Every change saves at once; there is no Save button. Switches make a little two-note sound up or down.
  • Volume sliders play a test sound (effects) or keep the music playing (music) so you hear the level while you drag.
  • The × (or the phone’s back button) goes back to wherever Settings was opened from.
  • Settings is also reachable from the pause button in every minigame (Sound and Graphics sections only).

The grown-up gate

A sum written in words (“fourteen minus six”), answered on a number pad. Easy for an adult, hard for a 5–8-year-old, and it doesn’t ask for any personal data such as a birth year. Three wrong answers lock it for 30 seconds. One correct answer opens the grown-up area until Settings is closed. Try it: tap Open grown-up settings in the phone.

The same gate appears before buying Gems, before opening any link that leaves the app (help page, privacy policy, store page), before linking an email, and before deleting the account.

Graphics

Picking the right quality automatically

First start-up

  1. Known devices: look the phone model up in a list shipped with the game (all iPhones and iPads, plus the ~300 most common Android phones) and use its tier.
  2. Unknown device: score it. Memory under 3 GB = Low; 3–5 GB = Medium; 6 GB or more = High. Fewer than 6 CPU cores caps it at Medium. Old GPUs on a block list are always Low. A very high-resolution screen (wider than 1200 pixels) on a Medium phone renders at 80% resolution.
  3. Check it: while the logo and the first story scene play, measure frame times for 3 seconds. If 1 frame in 10 takes longer than 33 ms, drop one tier. If a Low phone is easily fast enough (under 14 ms), raise it to Medium.
  4. Save the result. The player never sees any of this, just a game that runs smoothly.

While playing

  • If the city map stays under 25 fps for 15 seconds, drop one tier (once per session) and show a small toast: “We made the graphics lighter so things run smoother. You can change this in Settings.”
  • Never raise the tier by itself, so it can’t flip back and forth.
  • If the phone reports it is getting hot, switch to Low at 30 fps until it cools down.
  • A choice the player makes by hand always wins. Choosing Auto again turns all of this back on.
