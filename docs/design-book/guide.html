How Squishy Island works

Squishy Island is a cosy collecting game for kids aged about 5 to 11. Players collect squishies, fix up a run-down little island town one house at a time, and play short minigames with the squishies they own. The whole city is a cartoon 3D world seen from above at an angle (like a city-builder): players drag to move around, pinch to zoom in on the squishies, and tap buildings to go inside.

The core loop

One loop, repeated

  1. Play a minigameEach fun building has one. Five are endless and get harder the longer you last; three are level-based (Lost & Found, Bucket Brigade, Squish Breaker) with stars and new levels forever; Squeak Beat has 30 songs to unlock. The Squishy Testing Facility is a toy just for squishing.
  2. Earn coinsEvery run pays coins from its score, plus a milestone bonus for a great run. Puzzle levels pay for each star.
  3. Spend themOpen Mystery Boxes, renovate a house, build or upgrade a fun building, or buy decor.
  4. The city comes aliveNew squishies move into their house and start their idle animation on the map.
  5. Play betterMore and rarer squishies give minigame bonuses, so you earn more next time.
  6. Come back tomorrowNew daily quests with a Gem Challenge, and a visiting squishy hoping to move in.

Economy

Same time, same coins

Mystery Boxes cost 200 coins, Family Boxes 500 and Golden Boxes 1,000. Every minigame is tuned to pay about the same per minute: roughly 35 coins per minute for a good player. So a Golden Box takes about half an hour of play, around 12–15 runs, whichever games you like best.

Premium currency

Squish Gems: rare, but earnable

Gems are meant to feel special. A player who never pays can still earn them, but slowly: about 30 Gems a week for someone who plays most days. That is a Legendary from the Gem Shop roughly every 2–3 months, or an exclusive every 4. Coins stay the everyday currency.

How the Gem Challenge goal is set

  • Endless games: reach 90% of your own best score in that game (Sky Bounce: 90% of your best height). A new player’s goal is small; an expert’s is big. It is always a stretch, never impossible.
  • Squeak Beat: ★★★ on any track you have, or ★★ on the newest one.
  • Level games (Lost & Found, Bucket Brigade, Squish Breaker): win your next new level with ★★★, or raise any two levels to ★★★.
  • Unlimited tries all day. Only games the player has built are picked, never the same game two days running.
  • The quest board shows tomorrow’s game as a teaser, so there is a reason to come back.

Gem rules (kids and parents)

  • Gems never buy anything random: no boxes, no lucky draws.
  • Gems and coins never convert into each other.
  • Buying Gems needs a grown-up gate (not a tap-through). Each pack shows its real-money price, and the Gem Shop shows what an item would cost in money.
  • No “limited time, buy now!” timers or pressure pop-ups. The weekly front-shelf discount just sits quietly on the shelf.
  • Gem Shop exclusives can’t be traded, so trades between kids never involve real money.

Daily visits

Reasons to come back, never a punishment for staying away

Squishies never get sick, sad or leave. Missing a day only means missing that day’s bonus. Parents should never see a crying squishy because their child went on holiday, and kids should never feel they have to log in.

Squishies

8 families, 48 squishies, 5 rarities

Every family has exactly 6 squishies: 2 Common, 1 Uncommon, 1 Rare, 1 Epic and 1 Legendary. Six families are inspired by real squishy toys (dumplings, breakfast foods, pets, sweets, fruit, sea creatures) and two are made up (Sky Puffs and Forest Folk).

Collection pacing

Every box wins, the collection still takes years

The first half of the collection comes quickly on purpose, so the city fills up and the game feels generous. The long tail is the Legendaries, then levelling and Sparkly versions, which no player is expected to finish in under a year.

The Spare Shelf

  • A squishy you already own goes onto your Spare Shelf (it isn’t turned into something automatically). The one living in your house never leaves.
  • Each spare can be used three ways, and the player chooses:
    • Level up that squishy (it hugs its twin and they become one, stronger squishy)
    • Trade it at the Swap Meet Market (only spares can be traded)
    • Swap it for Squish Stars
  • So collectors level up, traders trade, and nothing is wasted.

Squishy levels 1–5

  • Spares needed per level: Common & Uncommon 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 (10 in total), Rare & Epic 1 / 1 / 2 / 2 (6), Legendary 1 / 1 / 1 / 1 (4).
  • Lv 2: a bigger, bouncier idle. Lv 3: an accessory (scarf, bow, tiny hat). Lv 4: +Cozy points for its house. Lv 5: it turns Sparkly (shimmering coat, golden name plate).
  • In Squish Breaker a levelled squishy’s ball hits +1 harder per level; elsewhere levels are just for show, so new players are never shut out.
  • Sparkly pulls: 1 box in 50 is already Sparkly, a lucky shortcut. Sparklies fill page two of the collection book.

Ads

Rewarded ads only, always the player’s choice

Where ads appear

  • Keep going: when a minigame run ends, a card offers “▶ Watch an ad” to continue once (1 more heart, +5 moves, a balloon float, and so on; each game says what you get). “No thanks” ends the run normally. Once per run. All 10 prototypes have this, with a pretend ad.
  • Free Box: one Mystery Box per ad, at most 3 a day, from a button in the Factory.
  • That’s all: no banner ads, no ads between levels, no ad when the app opens or a level starts, and no ads at all in the Squish Lab or the Market.

Rules for a kids’ app

  • Use only an ad network certified for children’s apps (Google Play’s Families Ads Program; Apple requires human-reviewed, kid-appropriate ads), with no personalised ads and no tracking.
  • Every ad is opted into with a clear button, is labelled “Ad”, and never pretends to be part of the game.
  • Every ad can be closed after 5 seconds (Google Play Families rule, rewarded ads included). Closing early just means no reward.
  • The “keep going?” card stays neutral: no sad squishies, no countdown, no “last chance”.
  • An ad-unlocked box still shows its odds before the ad.
  • A grown-up setting can switch ads off (for example, included with any purchase).

Progression

An open island

Nothing is locked. From the first minute the whole island is there to explore, and progress comes from fixing things up, building, decorating and collecting, never from waiting for something to unlock.

On day 1What the player finds
HousesAll 8 family houses, all broken (level 1). The Dumpling Steamhouse has Bao Bun living in it. A squishy from any family moves straight into its own house, broken or not.
Built and readyBounce Park (Sky Bounce), Squish Bistro (Bistro Rush), Squishy Post Office (Sort-a-Lot): three different games to choose from right away. Also the Squishy Factory and the Gem Mine.
Empty plotsEvery other fun building, the Swap Meet Market and the Squishy Testing Facility: a plot with a "Build me!" sign, built with coins whenever the player likes (about 10–20 minutes of play each).

Later houses cost more to fix up (each family has its own price multiplier), so the cheapest path runs Dumpling Steamhouse → Teapot Kitchen → Paw Palace → … on its own, and the tutorial and daily quests suggest what to try next, without ever forcing an order. A player can always go back and keep upgrading any house.

Why open? Players are 5–11: a chain of "this needs that house at level 3, which needs…" was confusing, and a single starting minigame was risky (a child who doesn't like it may stop before the others open). The 8 houses × 10 levels, the fun-building upgrades, decor and the 48-squishy collection give plenty of progress. Decided October 2026 (decisions.md §16).

House rules

  • Every house starts at level 1: broken (boarded windows, weeds, holes).
  • Each level costs coins (100 at level 2 up to 2,500 at level 10, more for later houses).
  • Some levels also need family squishies living there: 1 at levels 3-4, 2 at 5-6, 3 at 7-8, 4 at 9, 5 at 10.
  • Renovating plays a short "builders" animation: scaffolding, dust puff, ta-da.
  • Upgrade celebration: every time a house or fun building goes up a level (on the map or inside the house), confetti pops up around it and three little fireworks burst above it for about a second, with the ta-da sound and a buzz. Fewer pieces on lower graphics settings; with "reduced motion" a few stars just twinkle in place.
  • Inside each house is a room. Decor bought there adds Cozy points (minigame coin bonus for that family). Every decor item comes in 4 designs: Classic, Candy Pop, Mint Breeze and Starlight.

Fun building rules

  • Every fun building starts as an empty plot (level 0) with a "Build me!" sign.
  • Level 1 is bought with coins, any time (Bounce Park, Squish Bistro and the Post Office start built).
  • Levels 2-10 need a best score in that building’s minigame (for the two puzzle games: a number of levels beaten), then coins. Playing better makes the building look fancier.
  • The Factory levels up from boxes opened, the Market from trades finished.
  • Upgrades are purely visual plus small unlocks (new songs, extra seat, new box type, new lab tools). Building and every upgrade get the same confetti-and-fireworks celebration as houses.

The living city

Animation rules

  • Everything loops in place. Nothing walks across the map. Each loop is 2 to 4 seconds long, like a GIF.
  • Every squishy blinks about every 4 seconds, with random offsets so they never blink together.
  • One spot per squishy. Each squishy has a fixed spot around its house and one signature action (pool jumps, mowing, mail, window peeking, sunbathing, napping, swaying on the roof, floating).
  • The city breathes too: chimney smoke, fountain water, flags, spinning windmill, twinkling sparkles, glowing lanterns at higher levels.
  • The map camera looks at the town from the front at about 25–50°, so every building shows its front door and its squishies. Every building faces the camera. Drag pans, pinch/scroll zooms, double-tap zooms in. Zooming in lowers the camera angle so buildings look taller and squishies become visible.
  • Map bubbles: every building with a job has a round icon bubble floating above it so it is easy to find from far away. Fun buildings show their own icon (chef hat, badge, scissors...). Family houses show a little house in the family colour with the family emblem squishy on it. A small badge shows the level, a lock, or + for an empty plot.
  • On older phones or with "reduce motion" on, loops pause on a nice still frame.

Art style

How a squishy is made

  • 3D look: every squishy is a soft, puffy, rounded toy (like a real squishy) with a slightly glossy plastic finish. Each one starts as a flat design drawing that is “inflated” into 3D, so the outline you see on the cards is exactly the toy’s silhouette.
  • Shape: one soft, chunky silhouette that still reads when it is tiny on the city map. Flat bottom so it sits on the ground.
  • Cartoon finish: everything has a soft plum outline, like the original drawings. Buildings use flat cartoon (toon) shading in a few clean steps; squishies stay smooth and glossy like real squishy toys.
  • Alive: buildings gently breathe (a tiny squash and stretch), squishies blink and loop their idle action.
  • Face: two round dark eyes with a white highlight dot, pink blush ovals, a tiny mouth. Faces sit low on the body.
  • Moods use the same face: happy arcs for joy, little curves for sleeping, an "o" for surprise.
  • Legendaries get gold accents and two twinkling sparkles.

Before launch

The non-code checklist

Store rules, EU and country rules and the full document list are on the Google Play & legal page.

The nameThe game is called Squishy Island, and the Google Play title is “Squishy Island: Collect & Play” (exactly 30 characters, Google’s limit). The old working name Squishville was dropped because it is a Squishmallows toy line. “Squishy” is an everyday word, so it is safe to use but hard to own: before launch, have a lawyer run a trademark search (EU, Hungary, US), check the domain and social handles, and consider registering the name together with a logo. Also keep squishy designs clearly different from Squishmallows and other squishy brands.

Legal and stores

  • Lawyer review of the Gems, ads, free boxes and trading against COPPA, GDPR (kids) and the upcoming EU Digital Fairness Act.
  • Privacy policy (a parent version and a short kid version), terms of use, refund policy.
  • Store programme choice: Apple Kids Category and Google Designed for Families each restrict ads, analytics and links. Decide early: it changes which SDKs you may use.
  • Age ratings (IARC questionnaire on Google Play, Apple’s age rating). Expect PEGI 3 / “Everyone”, plus the in-game purchases notice.
  • Company and money: a company to publish under, developer accounts, tax/VAT setup for app-store sales, bank account.
  • Contracts with full rights for the composer, voice actors (Bao + narrator, every language), artists, and any sound libraries. The fonts used here (Bagel Fat One, Nunito, Patrick Hand) are open-licence and fine to ship.

People, content and launch

  • Playtests with real kids aged 5–11 (with parent consent forms) at every milestone, especially the first five minutes and the economy.
  • Parent page on a website: what the game is, how money, ads and data work, how to delete an account, a support email.
  • Support plan for lost towns, purchase problems and deletion requests (who answers, how fast).
  • Language: English only at launch. Keep every text in one string table from day one, so adding languages later is a translation job, not a rebuild.
  • Live content plan: new squishy families, seasonal events and visitor schedule for the first year.
  • Store page: icon, screenshots, a short video, description, keywords. Rules for advertising to children are strict (no “buy now” aimed at kids, influencer ads must be labelled).
  • Soft launch in one or two small countries first to tune retention and earnings before the big launch.
  • Test devices: a few old, cheap Android phones; that is where Low graphics and battery saver matter.
